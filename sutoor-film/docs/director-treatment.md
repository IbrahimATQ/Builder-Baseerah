## 2. Reading the poem

The poem is an elegy, but these verses widen from one death to all of us. The argument runs:

1. **Mortality (I).** We bury one another, and the latest of us walk on the skulls of the earliest. The ground itself is made of the dead. *Intensity 4/10: grave, contemplative.*
2. **The vanity of preparation (II).** We ready Mashrafi swords and long spears, yet fate kills us with no battle at all. Bitter irony. *5/10.*
3. **Time cannot be outrun (III).** We keep the fastest horses tethered close, ready, but they cannot save us from the *khabab* (the trotting gait) of the nights. Time is a horse that outruns horses. *6/10, motion.*
4. **Detachment (IV).** "It has grown light to me; I no longer care about calamities, because caring never did me any good." A withdrawal inward, a held breath. *3/10, intimate.*
5. **Wounds accumulate (V).** Time has shot me with losses until my heart lies in a sheath of arrowheads. *8/10, rising.*
6. **Resilience (VI).** So now, when arrows strike me, the blades break on the blades already lodged there. Pain has become armour. *10/10, climax, then resolution.*

## 4. Visual concept

One metaphor per hemistich, about a dozen shots, no literal illustration:

- **Ground and dust**: the dead beneath us (tombs, footsteps on rippled sand).
- **Steel and flame**: preparation and its futility.
- **Horse and sky**: readiness against the gallop of time.
- **Air and fabric**: detachment (a curtain breathing, sand leaving an open hand).
- **Arrows and stone**: accumulation, then endurance (a target dense with arrows; sea breaking on rock; a lone figure who does not move).

Recurring presence: a solitary human figure seen only in part (feet, hand, silhouette), never a face, so the viewer can stand in that place.

Camera: slow push-ins of 3–8 %, gentle lateral drift, no shaky handheld, no drone clichés. Dissolves fall only between hemistichs, never inside one.

## 6. Score (original; CC0 VSCO-2 CE samples)

The tonal centre is **E**, matched to the reciter's voice (pYIN median just below E). Maqam Kurd on E for the elegy, Hijaz on E for the wounds, and an open E–B fifth lit by G♯ for endurance.

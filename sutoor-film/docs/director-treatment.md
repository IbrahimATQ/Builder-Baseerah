## 2. Reading the poem

The poem is an elegy, but these verses widen from one death to all of us. The argument runs:

1. **Mortality (I).** We bury one another, and the latest of us walk on the skulls of the earliest. The ground itself is made of the dead. *Intensity 4/10: grave, contemplative.*
2. **The vanity of preparation (II).** We ready Mashrafi swords and long spears, yet fate kills us with no battle at all. Bitter irony. *5/10.*
3. **Time cannot be outrun (III).** We keep the fastest horses tethered close, ready, but they cannot save us from the *khabab* (the trotting gait) of the nights. Time is a horse that outruns horses. *6/10, motion.*
4. **Detachment (IV).** "It has grown light to me; I no longer care about calamities, because caring never did me any good." A withdrawal inward, a held breath. *3/10, intimate.*
5. **Wounds accumulate (V).** Time has shot me with losses until my heart lies in a sheath of arrowheads. *8/10, rising.*
6. **Resilience (VI).** So now, when arrows strike me, the blades break on the blades already lodged there. Pain has become armour. *10/10, climax, then resolution.*

## 4. Visual concept

One metaphor per hemistich, about a dozen shots, no literal illustration:

- **Clock and crowd (today)**: the poem is not a museum piece. A real clock's hands move over the burial of generations, and the crowd at Grand Central flows beneath its great clock, the living walking over those before them.
- **Steel and flame**: preparation and its futility.
- **Horse and train**: readiness, and the fastest thing we have built, against the gallop of the nights.
- **Air and fabric**: detachment (a curtain breathing, sand leaving an open hand).
- **Arrows and stone**: accumulation, then endurance (a target dense with arrows; sea breaking on rock; a lone figure who does not move).

Human presence: real people, never staged models. Commuters caught in passing, never posed and never a lingering close-up, so the viewer can stand in their place. Ancient and modern images alternate, so the eleventh-century verse speaks to today.

Camera: slow push-ins of 3–8 %, gentle lateral drift, no shaky handheld, no drone clichés. Dissolves fall only between hemistichs, never inside one.

## 6. Score (original; CC0 VSCO-2 CE samples)

The tonal centre is **E**, matched to the reciter's voice (pYIN median just below E). Maqam Kurd on E for the elegy, Hijaz on E for the wounds, and an open E–B fifth lit by G♯ for endurance.

| Section | Cue |
|---|---|
| Prologue | contrabass E pedal fades in; low piano E1, then B1; desert wind |
| I | cello open fifth E–B; the "sigh" F→E (viola) under «أَواخِرُنا» |
| II | E-minor colour, then F major over the E pedal on «وَتَقتُلُنا المَنونُ»; one muffled timpani stroke (drum tuned to E) |
| III | pizzicato *khabab*: a dotted trot that accelerates (0.52 → 0.36 s per stride); high violin B for the night |
| IV | withdrawal: one piano voice (E, B/G, F→E) under a held violin E5 |
| V | E timpani roll crescendo; tremolo cello; Hijaz F → G♯; horn enters |
| VI | arrival on E–B–E–G♯–B with horns; timpani stroke on «تَكَسَّرَتِ النِصالُ»; low piano octave |
| Coda | strings hold and fade; harp E–B; a last high piano E |

Mix: the voice sits 12–18 dB above the music during every word (34 dB in Act IV). The music is ducked by word timing, with a 2.8 kHz presence dip. Master: −14 LUFS integrated, −1 dBTP.

## 7. Typography

- Verses: **Amiri**, warm ivory #EFE6D2, 50 px on 1080p, line-height 1.75. Classical diwan layout in the lower scope matte: sadr on the right, ajuz on the left, never over the picture.
- Each hemistich appears 0.12 s before it is spoken (a 0.45 s fade with a 3 px blur-to-sharp); the whole bayt clears before the next verse.
- Title: «النِصالُ عَلى النِصالِ» in **Aref Ruqaa**, champagne #D8C49C, with a hairline rule and «أبو الطيّب المتنبّي» in Amiri.
- No outlines, no glow, no letter-spacing. Rendered by Chromium/HarfBuzz and checked with a still of every hemistich.

## 8. Format

The free source footage (Mixkit) is 1080p at best and often 720p. A full-screen 9:16 crop would need 1.8–2.7× upscaling, so the master is **16:9 1920×1080 with a 2.39:1 scope matte**. A **9:16 vertical edition** (4:5 picture window, stacked verses) is rendered from the same edit for phone feeds.

## 9. Final shot list (14 shots)

Cuts fall in the pauses between hemistichs. Dissolves are 0.25–0.7 s (1.2 s from black, 0.3 s into the coda). Hard cuts go into the horse, the lightning and the Alhambra, so the candle flame, the lightning's afterglow and the sea spray never ghost over the next image. The lightning is flash-limited to at most 2 strikes per second (photosensitivity safety; see the QC report). Every clip's licence was read from its own source page. Seven earlier Mixkit picks were dropped because they were under Mixkit's Restricted (personal-use) licence. After review, S1 (temple ruins) and S2 (feet on a beach) were replaced because they read as artificial. An independent shot-by-shot review then replaced S10 (particle sparks that read as CGI) and S7 (an unreadable curtain), and regraded S11, whose crushed blacks made the rocks look composited; S1, S2 and S6 now use real contemporary footage (a clock, Grand Central, a train), so the classical verse speaks to the present.
